## 2. VERIFIED PEER-REVIEWED REFERENCES REGISTER

### Category A: UAE Desert Plant Seed Ecophysiology & Photoblastism

#### Ref 1: *Cyperus conglomeratus* (Dune Sedge Germination & Light Requirement)
* **Citation:** El-Keblawy, A., Al Neyadi, S. S., Rao, M. V., & Al-Marzouqi, A. H. (2011). "Interactive effects of salinity, light and temperature on seed germination of sand dunes glycophyte *Cyperus conglomeratus* growing in the United Arab Emirates deserts." *Seed Science and Technology*, 39(2), 364–376.
* **DOI:** `10.15258/sst.2011.39.2.09`
* **Verified Claim:** *Cyperus conglomeratus* seeds exhibit a strict requirement for light to achieve maximum germination (virtually zero germination in total darkness), and germination is significantly inhibited by increasing salinity (NaCl), confirming the need for a shallow-depth, light-transmitting micro-environment.

#### Ref 2: *Haloxylon salicornicum* (Desert Shrub Germination, Temperature & Salinity)
* **Citation:** El-Keblawy, A., & Al-Shamsi, N. (2008). "Salinity, temperature and light affect seed germination of *Haloxylon salicornicum*, a common perennial shrub of the Arabian deserts." *Seed Science and Technology*, 36(3), 679–688.
* **DOI:** `10.15258/sst.2008.36.3.16`
* **Verified Claim:** Fresh *Haloxylon salicornicum* seeds germinate rapidly under cool-to-moderate thermal regimes (characteristic of UAE winter/spring rainfall events) and maintain higher germination percentages under light exposure than in darkness, demonstrating the transient soil seed bank strategy.

#### Ref 3: Desert Annual Dormancy & Chemical Regulators (*Eremobium aegyptiacum*)
* **Citation:** El-Keblawy, A., Bhatt, A., & Gairola, S. (2014). "Storage conditions affect dormancy and germination of the Arabian desert annual *Eremobium aegyptiacum*." *Journal of Arid Land*, 6(5), 598–606.
* **DOI:** `10.1007/s40333-014-0066-5`
* **Verified Claim:** Fresh seeds of *Eremobium aegyptiacum* exhibit deep innate physiological dormancy upon dispersal, requiring dry after-ripening and specific moisture/temperature cues; artificial chemical promoters have limited efficacy without adequate after-ripening and light.

### Category B: Date Palm Biochar & Arid Soil Amendment

#### Ref 4: Date Palm Frond Biochar Characterization & Techno-Economics (Khalifa University)
* **Citation:** Al-Rumaihi, A., McKay, G., & Mackey, H. R. (2022). "Performance, life cycle assessment, and economic comparison between date palm waste biochar and activated carbon derived from woody biomass." *Heliyon*, 8(12), e12388.
* **DOI:** `10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e12388`
* **Verified Claim:** Date palm agricultural waste (fronds and leaves) pyrolyzed under controlled temperatures produces a highly stable carbon adsorbent with favorable life cycle emissions and low production cost compared to imported commercial carbons, supporting local circular bioeconomy integration in Abu Dhabi.

#### Ref 5: Date Palm Biochar Surface Chemistry & Porosity
* **Citation:** Usman, A. R. A., Abduljabbar, A., Vithanage, M., Ok, Y. S., Ahmad, M., Ahmad, M., Elfaki, J., Abdulazeem, S. S., & Al-Wabel, M. I. (2015). "Biochar production from date palm waste: charring temperature induced changes in composition and surface chemistry." *Journal of Analytical and Applied Pyrolysis*, 115, 392–400.
* **DOI:** `10.1016/j.jaap.2015.08.016`
* **Verified Claim:** Increasing pyrolysis temperature from 300°C to 700°C significantly alters surface functional groups and pore volume; biochar produced in the 400–500°C range retains aromatic carbon networks and micro-porosity essential for soil conditioning and moisture retention in arid soils.
